# Why OpenRepurpose Matters

## The Problem
About 90% of drugs that enter clinical trials never reach patients. The ones that do cost roughly $2B and take 10–15 years. Repurposing — finding new indications for already-approved or shelved drugs — skips much of that risk because safety, dosing, and manufacturing are characterized. Yet today, repurposing is a hand-craft: a scientist with a hypothesis spends weeks pulling genetics, expression data, prior trial signals, and mechanism evidence into a single dossier — and that work gets repeated for every new hypothesis.

## What OpenRepurpose Does
Treats the repurposing dossier as a generated artifact. A (gene, disease) or (drug, target) query returns a structured evidence card with the rigor of a manual one, in minutes instead of weeks. Outputs are versioned and auditable — which matters for downstream regulatory and licensing conversations.

## Who Benefits
- Translational researchers at academic medical centers
- Small-cap pharma scouting in-license opportunities
- Foundations funding rare-disease repurposing trials
- Investigator-initiated trial groups

## Why Now
Open Targets, ChEMBL, ClinicalTrials.gov, and DepMap are usable as APIs. Repurposing-evidence generation is the kind of task LLMs do best: structured retrieval plus consistent synthesis across heterogeneous sources.

## The Bigger Picture
Repurposing is one of the highest-leverage levers in drug development. Even small reductions in dossier-prep time translate directly into more shots-on-goal for diseases that lack therapies — particularly rare and neglected ones where commercial economics don't justify a *de novo* program.
